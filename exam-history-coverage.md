# International history exam coverage · 16 November 2026

The atlas is suitable as a map and recall aid for many international-history questions. It is not a stand-alone source for the full International Affairs preliminary or written paper. BPSC prescribes broad themes rather than a finite list of events. This review checks examples and coverage gaps; it does not estimate exam frequencies, guarantee a mark range, or predict future questions.

- [BPSC 50th preliminary syllabus notice](https://bpsc.gov.bd/pages/psc-exams/৫০তম-বি-সি-এস-পরীক্ষা-২০২৫-এর-প্রিলিমিনারি-টেস্টের-mcq-type-সিলেবাস-299ca4-69568a5d35ce18e1c05ad0af). The International Affairs section has 25 marks. Its 5-mark world-history block also covers regional and international systems and geopolitics. The other blocks are security and power relations, current events, environmental diplomacy, and international organizations/economic institutions. The [accessible 50th syllabus reproduction](https://elasticbeanstalk-ap-southeast-1-051040323559.s3.amazonaws.com/livemcq-files/media/documents/50th_bcs_syllabus.pdf) was used for the block breakdown because the official attachment was not consistently reachable. Follow the notice for the examination you are actually taking.
- [BPSC written syllabus, subject 007, printed pp. 8–9](https://bpsc.gov.bd/sites/default/files/files/bpsc.portal.gov.bd/psc_exam/3c11cd64_6370_4e04_be9a_60f82b085597/SYLLABUS%20FOR%20BCS%20%28WRITTEN%29%20EXAMINATION.pdf). Historical context supports the written paper’s treatment of global and regional institutions, conflict and diplomacy, economics, and South Asia. The paper also requires short conceptual notes, analytical answers and problem solving; a timeline does not teach or assess all of those skills.

Question websites were used as **reproductions**, not as official keys or the factual authority. Row numbers can differ by set or publisher. The atlas uses original study summaries and links the underlying historical record where possible.

The written-syllabus comparison also identified gaps in the historical chains around Palestine and Syria, Cold War alliance and non-alignment, economic institutions (Bretton Woods versus Marrakesh), and South Asian diplomacy (Simla, Dhaka SAARC, and Farakka). The added records connect causes, dates, venues and outcomes; they do not describe live conflict or treaty status. Use **Timeline → Exam history only** for the curated shortlist. Some entries carry a past-paper cue; other entries were added because they supply useful syllabus context, not because they are claimed to have appeared on an exam.

For written answers, practise explaining what changed and why: for example, compare the legal purpose and outcome of Palestine resolutions 181, 242 and 338, or contrast the IMF/IBRD with the WTO. Continue checking dated official sources for current disputes, membership, treaty status and leadership. This atlas does not replace that work or subject 007 answer practice.
